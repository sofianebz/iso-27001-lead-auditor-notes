# 04 — Audit Planning

## 1. Relevant audit frameworks

Key references include:

- ISO/IEC 17021-1
- ISO/IEC 27006-1
- IAF MD 1
- IAF MD 4
- IAF MD 26
- IAF MD 28

These documents provide requirements or guidance relevant to management-system certification audits.

## 2. Audit team roles

### Lead Auditor

Responsible for coordinating the audit, including:

- planning
- opening meeting
- coordination of the audit team
- review of evidence
- evaluation of findings
- closing meeting
- audit reporting

### Auditor

Performs activities such as:

- interviews
- evidence review
- observations
- evaluation of conformity
- documenting findings

### Technical expert

Provides specialized technical knowledge when required.

### Observer

May observe the audit for training, supervision or other defined purposes without influencing audit conclusions.

## 3. Application and review

Before an audit, relevant information can include:

- organization context
- proposed scope
- applicable standards
- previous certifications or audits
- methodology
- locations
- complexity
- relevant risks
- estimated audit time

## 4. Audit programme

An audit programme defines the overall audit activities and can cover the certification cycle.

It should consider:

- scope
- frequency
- criteria
- resources
- scheduling
- previous findings
- changes in the organization
- changes in risk

## 5. Audit plan

The audit plan specifies the detailed execution of an audit.

Typical elements:

- audit objectives
- scope
- criteria
- audit team
- timetable
- processes/areas to be audited
- communication arrangements
- meetings
- reporting arrangements

A risk-based approach should be used when determining audit focus and resources.
