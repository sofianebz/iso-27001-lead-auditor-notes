# 03 — ISMS Implementation

## 1. Defining the ISMS scope

The scope should identify the activities, sites, organizational boundaries and assets covered by the ISMS.

When defining the scope, consider:

- organizational context
- interested parties
- legal and regulatory requirements
- interfaces and dependencies
- information and supporting assets

### Common scope problems

- Scope too broad and difficult to manage.
- Scope too narrow and critical systems excluded.
- Ambiguous boundaries between included and excluded activities.

## 2. Risk assessment

The risk assessment process should be defined and repeatable.

A practical sequence is:

1. Define risk assessment criteria.
2. Identify relevant assets and processes.
3. Identify threats and vulnerabilities/scenarios.
4. Assess likelihood and impact.
5. Determine the risk level.
6. Decide how the risk will be treated.
7. Reassess residual risk after treatment.

Typical risk treatment options:

- Avoid
- Modify / mitigate
- Transfer
- Accept

The exact methodology and scoring model should be defined by the organization.

## 3. Statement of Applicability (SoA)

The SoA documents the organization's treatment of the Annex A reference controls.

Typical information includes:

- control reference
- applicability
- justification
- implementation status

The SoA provides traceability between:

**Risks → Risk treatment → Controls → Implementation evidence**

## 4. Internal audit

Internal audits provide an independent assessment of the ISMS within the organization.

The audit program should consider:

- scope
- criteria
- objectives
- frequency
- importance of processes
- previous audit results
- relevant risks

Auditors should maintain appropriate objectivity and impartiality.

## 5. Management review

Management review evaluates whether the ISMS remains suitable, adequate and effective.

Inputs can include:

- previous management-review actions
- changes in context
- audit results
- nonconformities
- corrective actions
- performance measurements
- security objectives
- interested-party feedback
- risks and opportunities

Outputs can include:

- decisions concerning the ISMS
- changes to objectives or controls
- resource decisions
- action plans
- improvements
