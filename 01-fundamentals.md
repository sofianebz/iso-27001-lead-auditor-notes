# 01 — Fundamentals

## 1. ISO and standardization

- ISO = International Organization for Standardization.
- ISO was created in 1947.
- National Standards Bodies (NSBs) participate in ISO Technical Committees.
- The ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 develops the ISO/IEC 27000 family.
- Management system standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 use the harmonized management-system structure and follow the Plan-Do-Check-Act logic.

## 2. Accreditation vs certification

### Accreditation

Accreditation demonstrates the competence of a certification body (CB) to perform certification activities.

Relevant framework:
- ISO/IEC 17021-1
- Accreditation Bodies (AB)
- IAF Multilateral Recognition Arrangement (MLA)

### Certification

Certification provides assurance that an organization has demonstrated conformity with the applicable requirements of a standard through an audit performed by a certification body.

### Key distinction

- Accreditation concerns the competence of the certification body.
- Certification concerns the conformity of an organization or management system.

## 3. Certification audit cycle

A typical certification cycle includes:

1. Initial certification audit
   - Stage 1
   - Stage 2
2. Surveillance audits
3. Recertification audit

### Stage 1

Focuses on areas such as:
- ISMS design
- documentation
- scope
- readiness for Stage 2

### Stage 2

Focuses on:
- implementation
- operational effectiveness
- objective evidence of conformity

## 4. Audit findings

Common classifications include:

- Major nonconformity
- Minor nonconformity
- Opportunity for improvement
- Positive observation

### Correction vs corrective action

**Correction** addresses the immediate problem.

**Corrective action** addresses the underlying cause in order to prevent recurrence.

## 5. Normative vs informative

- Normative requirements are requirements that must be satisfied where applicable.
- Informative material provides guidance and context.

ISO/IEC 27002 is used as guidance for information security controls; ISO/IEC 27001 contains the requirements for the ISMS.

## 6. Revision, amendment and corrigendum

- **Revision:** a new edition of the standard.
- **Amendment:** a formal modification to an existing standard.
- **Corrigendum:** correction of an identified publication error.
